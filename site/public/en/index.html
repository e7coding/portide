Security

Private by design, not by promise

No accounts, no analytics, nothing of yours on our servers. The privacy comes from how Portide is built, and the code is open for anyone to check.

  • Keys that stay on the deviceEach device makes its own Ed25519 identity key on first launch and keeps it in the system keychain.
  • Pairing nobody can fakeThe code is checked with CPace, a password-authenticated key exchange. A wrong code or a man in the middle simply fails.
  • Encrypted on the wire and on diskQUIC with TLS 1.3 between devices; message text and file names are encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 in the local database.
  • Remove a device, lock it outTaking a device out of your circle replaces the circle’s keys.

Connections

At home or away, it finds a way

On the same Wi‑Fi, devices find each other and connect directly, so speed depends only on your network. Elsewhere, they meet through a relay and try to open a direct path. Where UDP is blocked, they fall back to TCP on their own.

The relay server is free and open source. Run it on any small Linux server, or with Docker.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is Portide free?

On Mac, Windows, Linux and the command line, yes: free and open source, and you can run your own relay for free. The iPhone and iPad app’s price will be on its App Store page when it launches. If we ever offer an official relay with a price, we’ll say so clearly before anything costs money.

Do I need an account, or the internet?

No account at all. On the same Wi‑Fi your devices find each other and connect directly, without going through the internet. You only need a relay when your devices are on different networks.

Why does my iPhone only receive while Portide is open?

iOS doesn’t let apps keep network connections open in the background. Your iPhone receives while Portide is open or just moved to the background; anything sent meanwhile waits on the other device and arrives the next time you open Portide.

What can a relay see?

Your device’s public IP address, a routing ID (a hash of the device’s public key), when connections start and end, and how many bytes pass. It can’t see file contents, file names, text, device names or who is in your circle, and it doesn’t store anything it forwards.

Is it safe on public Wi‑Fi?

Yes. Everything is encrypted end to end. Devices announce themselves with names that change every 10 minutes and that only your own devices recognize, so others on the network can tell Portide is running but not whose device it is.

Where do received files go?

On a computer, to the folder you choose, as ordinary files. On iPhone and iPad, into Portide’s folder in the Files app, and photos and videos into your photo library if you allow it.

What if I lose a device?

Remove it from your circle on any other device. That replaces the circle’s keys, so the lost device can’t connect or read anything sent afterwards.

What about Android?

It’s on the roadmap. Today Portide runs on macOS, Windows and Linux, with iPhone and iPad coming to the App Store soon.
